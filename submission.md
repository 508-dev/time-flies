# Concept

> A description of your concept in 500 words or less. Explain how your way of thinking about or experimenting with time reorients imagination, behavior, or decision-making.

Live web demo: https://timeflies.508.dev/

This application demonstrates a method of thinking about the subjective experience of time, and how that can change for an individual based on the activity the individual is doing. 

In 01938, Herbert E. Ives and G. R. Stilwell demonstrated the existence of relativistic time dilation, providing physical proof of the relativity of time.

However, there's a second factor to the relativity of time: individuals have a sense for time, but it's inaccurate compared to even simple clocks, and changes moment to moment. An example is demonstrated by the adage "time flies when you're having fun." One hour to a human waiting in a doctor's office "feels a lot longer" than one hour of playing basketball. Time relativity also differs from individual to individual in the same situation. Twenty minutes feels very different to humans of the same age in the same restaurant, when one human is hungry and waiting for their meal, and the other human is in the kitchen preparing that meal while being shouted at by an angry chef. Ten minutes feels very different to the adult human driving a car than it does to the child human riding in it.

This project represents a portion of this concept visually. The project allows the user to input activities alongside their time compression factor, and then represents those activities against a 3d graph that's in the shape of the modern human understanding of a black hole as of 02026 (popularized in the 02014 film "Interstellar"). 

In this graph, time compression factors will map against entities orbiting a black hole. Objects in the "corona" represent a compression factor of greater than or equal to 1. If waiting in a dentist office for an hour "feels like forever," the compression factor would be 3.0, where one hour feels like three hours. If waiting at a traffic light for one minute feels like one minute, the compression factor is 1. Activities with a compression factor of less than 1 are closing in on the event horizon of the black hole. They are a part of the accretion disk. 

A path to fulfillment is finding ways to "get into the zone," a state of experience where time "falls away" and an individual becomes one with an activity. This could be considered a singularity, a place outside of time and individual experience, where the ego has dissolved. This is invisible to the outside world, just as the outside world is invisible to the individual when they're in this "zone," which is represented by the event horizon of the black hole. 

As a user lists activities and their compression ratios, the hope is that individuals will have a better understanding of the kind of activities that can get them close to, or into, "the zone." Paradoxically, reference time passes more quickly, but the subjective experience stretches into infinity, similar to if an individual fell into a black hole.
